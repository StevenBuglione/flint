# Low-pause collector design (Astra, 2026-11-25)

Brief: `coordination/astra-gengc-brief.md`. Read-only design at 5034f892. Reviewed and corrected by Gemini 4 Neon in `gengc-review-neon.md`; **where they differ, the review's corrected slice plan governs.** Links to `/home/olfa/...` point at local reference clones (bdwgc, mmtk-core) or the pinned druntime.

**Build B first, on a clean port of the pinned druntime collector.** Petclinic’s best opportunity is to stop rediscovering roughly 5,500 permanent objects per collection. A stopped young-generation mark can do that without moving objects, changing D, or introducing concurrent marking. I would use **35–55 µs on `/nope` and 55–85 µs on petclinic** as engineering targets for ordinary minors, conditional on the dirty-page and suspension costs below. Full collections will still take hundreds of microseconds. BDWGC already supplies a capable C collection engine, but adapting D’s mutable block attributes, array ABI, finalizers and fiber roots makes it the longer route to a trustworthy HTTP measurement. Neither design yet establishes a win against Go. This is a read-only design against `5034f892`; no builds or benchmarks were run.

## A. What BDWGC supplies and what Flint must adapt

**To the owner’s question:** We can use BDWGC’s allocator, non-moving conservative marker, incremental scheduler, virtual dirty bits and thread-local free lists. Linking `libgc` alone leaves ordinary D `new`, array append, `GC.query`, suspended fibers and module unloading under druntime’s contract. A complete implementation is feasible with a D registry adapter **and collector-side integration**. A wrapper around public `GC_malloc` and `GC_gcollect` calls cannot implement every operation faithfully. **Threads and fibers are the decisive integration cost.** BDWGC’s pthread stopper captures native threads and registers. Use the registry’s early thread hook, `initThread`/`cleanupThread`, and `GC_register_my_thread` with matched ownership on unregister. That alone misses D TLS and dormant fiber stacks. Worse, a pthread interrupted on a fiber can have an SP outside its native stack. Registering inactive ranges alone does not fix the active stack bound. For a production A, I would give **druntime sole ownership of mutator suspension** in the pinned BDWGC port. Replace BDWGC’s mutator stop/resume and native-stack walk at its stopped-mark boundary. Eagerly push the ranges from `thread_scanAllType` while its suspension precondition holds. This covers active and saved contexts, registers, starting threads and TLS. Retain BDWGC’s own marker coordination and thread-local free-list roots. Audit lock order at attach, detach and fiber switch. An alternative using `GC_set_push_other_roots` with BDWGC’s stopper needs a switch-safe registry covering active stack bounds, suspended stacks, saved registers, D TLS and mappings until removal. ## B. A stopped, sticky young-generation mark

Start `sys/gc/sticky_collector.d` from approved druntime **v2.114.0**, preserving the LDC-specific compatibility lines, allocator, parallel full marker, D roots, arrays and finalization. Register `flint-sticky` beside the existing factories under `FlintCollector`. Boot already imports the linker anchor. First measure **full-only parity**. D1’s petclinic mark took 1.07 ms against 0.52 ms in the same diagnostic. Leave D1’s allocation caches out of the initial port. Cached slots can survive a minor in TLS before the application first receives them. [Existing factories](/home/olfa/flint-work/worktrees/agy-plan/packages/arena/source/flint/arena/sys/gc/collector.d:260) · [Boot selection](/home/olfa/flint-work/worktrees/agy-plan/boot/core/flint-boot/source/flint/boot/package.d:51) · [Measured regression](/home/olfa/flint-work/worktrees/agy-plan/docs/spring/phase1/gc.md:318)

1. **Allocation and age.** Add `sys/gc/generation.d`. Per pool, keep an allocated-slot young bitmap, sticky survivor marks, and a list of pages containing new allocations. Track large-block extents separately. Initialize age and clear stale marks before publication. Explicit free and reuse reset all slot metadata. Prefer young-only pages within existing size classes and segregate pointer-bearing from atomic pages where economical. Mixed pages remain valid.
2. **Minor.** Under the collector and root locks, stop D threads. Scan all static roots, root values, ranges, registers, TLS and active/suspended stacks. For each dirty or always-rescanned old page, scan allocated, old, pointer-bearing slots intersecting it, including continuations of multi-page objects. Follow interior pointers using druntime’s block lookup. Newly reached young objects enter the worklist once. Drain to closure, then promote **every** survivor in place by retaining its mark and clearing its young bit. Thus every old-to-young edge surviving this collection ends at an old object when execution resumes. This borrows StickyImmix’s sticky-mark idea. MMTk itself uses a mutator object barrier and can copy objects. [StickyImmix nursery trace](/home/olfa/flint-work/reference/mmtk-core/src/plan/sticky/immix/global.rs:273) · [MMTk remembered-set barrier](/home/olfa/flint-work/reference/mmtk-core/src/plan/generational/barrier.rs:73)
3. **Dirty epochs.** Add `sys/gc/dirty_pages.d`. First implement Linux soft-dirty with actual OS page size and batch reads. **Clear once at a full collection**, while stopped. On each minor read only old pointer-bearing spans and rescan the accumulated dirty pages. This avoids paying `clear_refs` every minor, at the price of a growing remembered set. On capable hosts, register eligible spans with userfaultfd async WP, initially arm them, then use `PAGEMAP_SCAN` with `PAGE_IS_WRITTEN`, `PM_SCAN_WP_MATCHING` and `PM_SCAN_CHECK_WPASYNC`. Continue from `walk_end` until the entire range is covered. Arm newly promoted pages before resume. Querying `PAGE_IS_SOFT_DIRTY` alone cannot reset a selected range. Changes of scan kind, mapping or ownership invalidate cleanliness. [Current soft-dirty implementation](/home/olfa/flint-work/worktrees/agy-plan/packages/arena/source/flint/arena/sys/gc/softdirty.d:52) · [Linux scan-and-rearm protocol](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/mm/pagemap.html)
4. **Sweep and triggers.** Freeze the young death set before resuming. Reuse druntime’s finalizer and sweep discipline under the allocation lock, restricted to dead young slots. Clear thread block caches before reuse. Old garbage waits for a major. Begin with a full GC. Trigger minors on allocation slow paths after consuming the young byte budget. Derive that budget from actual free headroom under `heapSizeFactor:3` and fixed 1 MiB pool increments, allowing dedicated large allocations. A pool increment is not a nursery limit. Force a full mark on explicit collect, allocation failure, old-generation pressure, or a maximum initial interval of 32 minors. Shorten that interval if dirty scanning approaches the measured full-mark cost. Full marking clears sticky state, traces from roots and rebuilds ages. [Pool settings](/home/olfa/flint-work/worktrees/agy-plan/packages/arena/source/flint/arena/sys/gc/fixed_pools.d:2) · [druntime resume/sweep boundary](/home/olfa/flint-work/vibe/flint/.tools/dmd2/src/druntime/src/core/internal/gc/impl/conservative/gc.d:3419)

## Pause, throughput and RSS budget

The measured root volumes imply a streaming component of **18 µs** for gs-rest (`461 KB × 0.04 ns/B`) and **34 µs** for petclinic (`838 KB × 0.04 ns/B`). Petclinic’s 3.33 MB live includes 2.1 MB of pointer-free JDBC arenas. At 5,900 first touches, 94% permanent, skipping clean old objects could save `5,900 × .94 × 50 ns ≈ 277 µs`. The remaining 354 touches cost about 18 µs. The census says **12 KB retained after 3,000 requests**. It does not measure 12 KB surviving per request or per minor. `/nope` needs its own survivor census. ## Correctness, landing slices and decision gate

**The safety invariant is that every possible source of a young reference is scanned, proved unchanged since promotion, or explicitly retained through publication.** Treat old objects with unobservable writes as Ruby RGenGC treats “shady” objects and rescan them every minor. For asynchronous kernel writers, rescanning alone is insufficient because stopping D threads does not stop DMA or io_uring. Use owned native or genuinely pointer-free I/O buffers. Where a completion can publish D pointers, strongly root the destination and every possible referent **before submission** through the original operation’s final completion, then publish under synchronization and mark the destination dirty. A cancel CQE is insufficient. Require quiescence or staging where referents cannot be enumerated. A full mark cannot repair a concurrent unrooted kernel publication either. BDWGC can omit pure atomic pages from `MPROTECT_VDB` protection under compatible page geometry. Linux `clear_refs` still affects the process, including atomic mappings.
