### Why Go has an advantage

Go marks concurrently. Compiler write barriers preserve reachability while application code mutates pointers. Per-P allocation caches make ordinary small allocations cheap. Go still stops execution at the boundaries of marking, and allocating goroutines can pay mark-assist CPU. For scale, a published two-P trace with a 1 MB live heap shows individual stops of **20 and 19 µs** in one cycle and **37 and 21 µs** in another. Those are examples, not a bound or a measurement of this comparator. Heap size alone cannot predict the pauses. Flint’s measured stops are about **150 µs on `/nope` and 300 µs on the 400**. [Go collector design](https://go.dev/doc/gc-guide) · [Go per-P allocator](https://go.dev/src/runtime/mcache.go) · [Two-P trace](https://github.com/golang/go/issues/19378) · [Flint tail join, Result 5](/home/olfa/flint-work/coordination/notes/web-http-tail-join.md:111)

Flint allocates **52.16 MB/s** on `/nope` at 10,000/s. I found no end-to-end Go bytes/request or GC-cycle count for that comparator in the supplied evidence. The sweep explicitly records Go’s collection count as `None`. So **pause length is an architectural advantage for Go, and frequency is a demonstrated lever for Flint. The between-language frequency and allocation ratios are unknown.** At the next diagnostic window, take fixed-phase deltas of Go’s `/gc/heap/allocs:bytes` per completed request, `/gc/cycles/total:gc-cycles` per second, and `/sched/pauses/total/gc:seconds`. Include `net/http` and reconnects. A handler-only allocation count would miss both. ### What the arithmetic supports

At the quoted Go medians, the targets are **129.69 µs** and **212.85 µs**. The gate ultimately uses the paired ratio’s upper 99% confidence bound. Paused wall fraction is `collections/s × mean stop`. Request exposure is larger because a stop catches work already in flight and delays a burst behind it. On posidon, `/nope` lost `67 × 150 µs = 1.005%` of wall time, yet **4.3% of all requests** overlapped GC, including 88% of the slowest percentile. On the 400, counting the accept-to-read interval raises tail overlap to about 93%. Accept itself was prompt. A practical design budget is **under 0.5% of requests delayed by GC**, leaving roughly half a percentile for ordinary slow requests. That is a target to test, not a requirement that every overlapping request misses its deadline. [Burst and span definitions](/home/olfa/flint-work/coordination/notes/web-http-tail-join.md:7) · [O3 overlap and accept trace](/home/olfa/flint-work/coordination/notes/web-http-tail-join.md:115)

### Ranked plan

1. **Proof-harness and web-http — establish the remaining gap.** Finish B5, then measure B6 with the already-approved factor 3. For each error path, report fixed-phase cycles/s, pause distribution, bytes/request, and deadline misses among requests free of both a pause and its backlog. If those requests alone exhaust 1%, prioritize dispatch and queueing CPU. The owner already authorized the validation rerun with saturation checked in the scored phase. [Owner decisions](/home/olfa/flint-work/worktrees/agy-plan/docs/spring/phase1/decisions.md:926)
2. **web-http and m7 — pool private exchange storage.** Use a bounded per-worker pool with one checkout per *in-flight exchange*. Clear headers, attributes, references and oversized buffers. Recycle only after async dispatch and final I/O completion, including cancellation. Give user code a fresh GC façade where needed. A retained reference must never expose the next request. This is a multi-day ownership and security review with bounded retained RSS. The gross savings are 3,408 B on 404 and 2,384 B on 400, less façade costs. Seek owner approval for any change to observable post-exchange behavior. ### Larger designs and rule limits

| Design | Feasibility, cost and recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Idle or budgeted GC** | An idle reactor invoking druntime GC still stops both reactors. A bounded, process-wide idle trigger could help bursty traffic. Sustained load offers no guaranteed gap, deferral consumes RSS, and stock marking cannot yield to a time budget. Medium effort with uncertain benefit. |
| **Local allocation or local heaps** | Thread caches are feasible and already prototyped. They spend bounded per-thread memory to reduce allocator locking. Independent local collection is unsafe when ordinary D references cross reactors through caches, `shared` or `immutable`. Enforcing isolation would require ownership restrictions, copying or handles across boundaries, with API, CPU and memory costs. [GC design and D1](/home/olfa/flint-work/worktrees/agy-plan/docs/spring/phase1/gc.md:238) |
| **Concurrent or incremental mark** | Software card marking needs coverage of every pointer write, including foreign writes. Ordinary D stores supply no barrier. OS page protection or Linux soft-dirty tracking is feasible with a final root and dirty-page rescan, safe handling of kernel-written memory, and STW fallback. The approved D3 prototype already worsened local gs-rest p99 from **0.91–1.06 to 2.65–2.75 ms**. Faults, rescans and lock waits matter on two CPUs. High engineering and safety cost, extra metadata and allocation headroom. Revisit only after measuring a small dirty set and fixing the slower stopped mark. [Prototype results and failure analysis](/home/olfa/flint-work/worktrees/agy-plan/docs/spring/phase1/gc.md:294) |
| **Request arenas** | Use stack or bounded arena storage for private parsing, routing, tokens and scratch. This is permitted today and reduces GC traffic directly. Even proven non-escape does **not** authorize handing arena-backed DTOs or strings to user code under ADR-030 §4. Changing that rule needs an owner ADR and lifetime enforcement across callbacks, fibers and retained references. [ADR-030 §4](/home/olfa/flint-work/worktrees/agy-plan/docs/adr/ADR-030-flint-is-a-spring-port.md:117) |
| **Precise GC** | Cheap diagnostic switch. Type bitmaps can reduce false retention and scanning, with metadata overhead and a need to audit manually typed buffers. Real reachable objects still incur first touches. An earlier same-parallelism comparison reduced cycles from 872 to 775 but increased total pause from 0.91 to 0.95 s. Low priority alongside the measured negatives for root trimming, prefetch, fork, permanent regions and larger pools. [GC matrix](/home/olfa/flint-work/worktrees/agy-plan/docs/spring/phase1/gc.md:21) · [Section 3b](/home/olfa/flint-work/worktrees/agy-plan/docs/spring/phase1/gc.md:318) · [D precise scanning](https://dlang.org/spec/garbage.html#precise_gc) |

**Fairness.** Keep both comparators. Go’s 404 constructs a timestamped, path-dependent error body and sends it chunked. Its invalid-owner handler decodes and validates before database access, and it closes every 400. Matching close-after-400 alone moved Go’s diagnostic p99 from **121 to 210 µs**. Go takes a shorter internal route, which ADR-033 explicitly permits Flint to do too. The legitimate advantage is avoiding framework machinery whose result is already known. The certification criterion remains the interleaved venus upper bound under 0.99 on **each** error workload.
